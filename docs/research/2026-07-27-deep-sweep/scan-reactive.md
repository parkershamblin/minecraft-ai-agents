**SwiftSage (arXiv 2305.17390; NeurIPS 2023).** The clearest published fast/slow switch policy. SWIFT = Flan-T5-large (770M) fine-tuned on 62,083 oracle-trajectory action-prediction examples with K=10 action history; SAGE = GPT-4 in two prompting stages — planning (five fixed questions: objects needed, subgoals, what's done, current focus, "have I made mistakes?") and grounding (subgoals → action-template sequence, emitted as an **action buffer**). SWIFT acts by default; four triggers hand control to SAGE: (1) **stuck** — 5 consecutive zero-reward steps; (2) **invalid** — SWIFT's proposed action is illegal in the current env; (3) **critical** — a decision like submitting the final answer; (4) **exception** — the observation reports an anomaly. SAGE's buffer then executes open-loop; control reverts to SWIFT when the buffer empties or after 2 consecutive invalid buffer actions. Arbitration is therefore temporal, not per-step voting — the buffer is a commitment window that prevents oscillation. ScienceWorld: 84.68 vs ReAct 36.43 / Reflexion 45.34, at 757 tokens/action vs 1,971/2,983. Survives your stack: the trigger set is model-free; the 770M fast policy is exactly consumer-GPU SFT territory (converges with MINDcraft's success-filtered SFT already in your shortlist).

**Talker-Reasoner (arXiv 2410.08328, DeepMind).** Talker (System 1) and Reasoner (System 2) are both Gemini 1.5 Flash (Reasoner CoT-prompted), coupled only through a **shared-memory belief state**. The Talker always answers using the *latest available* belief — deliberately acting on stale beliefs to keep latency low; the Reasoner updates beliefs asynchronously. The wait policy is state-keyed, not per-query: the Talker waits for the Reasoner **only when the belief marks the interaction as a "planning" phase**; the authors admit adaptive waiting is future work. No quantitative eval (qualitative sleep-coaching demo), no latency numbers, no conflict arbitration beyond the phase gate. The architecture (act-on-last-belief + phase-gated blocking) is model-agnostic and survives 8B; the evidence is thin.

**Cradle (arXiv 2403.03186, GPT-4V/GPT-4o).** Six modules (information gathering, self-reflection over up to 8 sampled video frames for success detection, task inference, skill curation as Python code, action planning, memory). The honest headline for your question: **Cradle pauses RDR2 while the LMM thinks** and unpauses to execute — it does not solve real time, it removes it. Combat is handled by a pre-scripted composite `fight()` skill because "the action frequency may not be high enough to defeat an opponent"; the paper concedes GPT-4V latency "makes it harder… to deal with time-sensitive events." Frontier-model-dependent throughout; the transferable piece is that even at frontier scale, time-critical behavior was moved into **code-side skills**, i.e., your mineflayer reflexes are the proven pattern.

**DEPS (arXiv 2302.01560, Minecraft; Codex code-davinci-002 for planning).** Replanning is **failure-triggered only**: the controller finishes or fails the current sub-goal, then a Describer distills state (inventory, biome) to text, an Explainer diagnoses the precondition that failed, and the planner revises. **No mid-sub-goal interruption exists.** The Selector — a trained network predicting remaining steps-to-completion per candidate goal — picks among parallel sub-goals and adds +22.3–32.6% success over fixed orderings. ObtainDiamond 0.59% (60% with oracle controller — the controller, not the plan loop, was the bottleneck). The loop is model-agnostic; the Selector is a small supervised model your ledger could train (predicted-horizon per verb+target is exactly what your event history contains).

**SwarmBrain (arXiv 2401.17749, StarCraft II — true real time, no pausing).** Two layers: the Overmind Intelligence Matrix (LLM, "state-of-the-art" — the paper used GPT-3.5-turbo-class models; latency motivates the design) issues macro strategy (expansion, army composition, attack timing); the **Swarm ReflexNet is a condition-response finite state machine with no LLM in the loop**, handling unit-level micro (engage/retreat-type reflexes). Arbitration is **by decomposition**: the layers own disjoint action granularities (macro orders vs unit micro), so conflicts largely can't arise — the same move as your busy-state arbitration table. Results: 100% wins vs built-in AI through Medium, 76% at Hard (30 matches, Automaton LE). Fully survives your stack — the LLM layer is prompt-only and the reflex layer is code.

**TextStarCraft II / Chain of Summarization (arXiv 2312.11865, NeurIPS 2024).** The best evidence on amortizing LLM latency in real time: single-frame summarization compresses observations; **multi-frame summarization aggregates K frames per LLM query, and each inference emits a K-length action queue that the environment pops one action per step while the next inference runs** — a producer-consumer overlap, not an interrupt system. Results vs Level-5 built-in AI: GPT-3.5-turbo-16k 11/20, GPT-4-Turbo 12/20, and critically **fine-tuned Qwen-1.8B 8/20 and Qwen-7B 9/20** (untuned Llama-2-70B failed) — small local models close the gap with SFT, on par with Gold-league humans (Qwen-1.8B 5/10 vs a Gold player). Directly survives — and strengthens the case for — your ledger→SFT exporter. Related: LLM-PySC2 (arXiv 2411.05348) reports an **asynchronous query architecture with constant latency regardless of agent count** — the multi-agent version of the same overlap.

**AdaPlanner (arXiv 2305.16653, GPT-3/3.5) — resume-vs-replan evidence.** Distinguishes **in-plan feedback** (observation consistent with the plan → keep executing, no LLM planner call) from **out-of-plan feedback** (deviation → refine the plan **from the failure point**, not from scratch). Refine-from-breakpoint beat full replanning on both success and token cost (ALFWorld ~91% with GPT-3.5 using far fewer demonstrations than baselines). Together with DEPS (finish-the-sub-goal-then-replan) and SwiftSage (buffer persists until invalid), the literature is consistent: **default to resume; replan only on diagnosed deviation.**

**Robotics background (one paragraph).** Inner Monologue (arXiv 2207.05608, PaLM/InstructGPT scale) shows closed-loop replanning driven by injected textual feedback — success detectors and scene descriptions appended to the prompt — i.e., trigger-based re-deliberation, but with frontier models and robot-speed (slow) environments. Code-as-Policies (arXiv 2209.07753) puts reactivity **inside generated code** (control loops, conditionals), so the LLM writes the reflex once and inference leaves the critical path — the ancestor of both Cradle's `fight()` and your executor reflexes. Treat both as pattern precedent, not scale evidence.

**Serialized/shared LLM backends (your Q5 — there is now a real literature).** Autellix (arXiv 2502.13965, evaluated **on LLaMA-3.1-8B**) treats each agent as a program issuing a DAG of LLM calls and schedules by **program-level attained service** (PLAS; ATLAS for multi-threaded programs uses critical-path service), discretized into MLFQ levels, **preempting at decode-step boundaries** with wait-to-service anti-starvation — 4–15× throughput over vLLM FCFS. Kairos (arXiv 2508.06948) does workflow-aware **agent-level** (not request-level) scheduling on vLLM for many agents sharing one model. Parrot (OSDI'24) passes application-level dependency hints to the server. None of this exists in Ollama — but the policies port to *your* dispatcher: schedule villager deliberations by least-attained-service, and let threat-triggered requests preempt queue position (not in-flight decodes).

## Design rules extracted

1. **Fast path acts by default; the LLM is an exception handler.** Evidence: SwiftSage (84.68 @ 62% fewer tokens), SwarmBrain (76% vs Hard AI with FSM micro), Cradle forced into scripted `fight()` even on GPT-4V.
2. **Proven trigger set for off-cycle deliberation: (a) N-consecutive-failures/zero-progress (SwiftSage K=5; your 3-refusal abandon-and-repropose is the same trigger, independently converged), (b) proposed-action-invalid, (c) exception/anomaly in observation, (d) critical/irreversible decision, (e) sub-goal completion or failure (DEPS).** Nobody reports value from generic "world changed" triggers; all earned triggers are *progress-referenced*, not stimulus-referenced. Health-drop/new-hostile triggers appear only as FSM *reflex* conditions (SwarmBrain), never as LLM triggers.
3. **Prevent oscillation with commitment windows, not per-step voting.** SwiftSage's SAGE buffer runs until empty or 2 invalid actions; DEPS never interrupts a sub-goal; AdaPlanner ignores in-plan feedback. Arbitration by *time-slice* (slow layer owns a window) or by *action-space decomposition* (SwarmBrain: macro vs micro) — never simultaneous authority over one actuator.
4. **Resume beats replan-from-scratch.** AdaPlanner refine-from-breakpoint; DEPS revises the remainder of the plan with the Explainer's diagnosis; SwiftSage re-enters fast mode with history intact. No paper reports full-replan winning.
5. **Amortize latency with action queues emitted per inference, consumed per tick.** TextSC2's K-frame summarization + K-action queue is the only tested bridge for true real time without pausing; your latest-intent-wins consumer is already the right sink for it — the missing piece is multi-action intents.
6. **The fast layer is (in proven order): scripted code/FSM (SwarmBrain, Cradle, Code-as-Policies) > small SFT model (SwiftSage 770M; TextSC2 Qwen-1.8B) > same model prompted cheaply (Talker-Reasoner).** No system runs a second *large* model fast.
7. **Fast layer reads a belief the slow layer writes, and acting on stale beliefs is a feature.** Talker-Reasoner's explicit design; phase-gated waiting only for plan-critical moments.
8. **On a shared backend, schedule by least-attained-service per agent and preempt only at safe boundaries.** Autellix (8B-tested, 4–15× vs FCFS), Kairos agent-level scheduling. For Ollama: implement at your dispatcher — priority queue where interrupt-triggered deliberations jump ahead of routine ticks, and a villager's queued-but-stale tick is dropped (you already have latest-intent-wins).

## Frontier-gap verdicts

- **SwiftSage switch policy:** survives fully — triggers are heuristics; fast path is a 770M SFT model (your GPU trains it; your ledger is the oracle-trajectory source).
- **TextSC2 queue+summarize:** survives — its *strong* results are fine-tuned Qwen-1.8B/7B, i.e., below your model class.
- **SwarmBrain FSM reflex layer:** survives trivially (no LLM); you already built it.
- **DEPS loop:** survives; the Selector needs a small trained horizon model (ledger-trainable). Planner quality at 8B is the risk, not the loop.
- **AdaPlanner resume-from-breakpoint:** survives (GPT-3.5-era; mechanism is prompt/state management).
- **Talker-Reasoner:** architecture survives; evidence is qualitative-only — adopt the belief-state pattern, don't cite it as proof.
- **Cradle:** does NOT transfer — pauses the game, GPT-4V-dependent; its lesson (reflexes as code) you already have.
- **Autellix/Kairos policies:** concepts port to your dispatcher today; the serving engines themselves assume vLLM, not Ollama.
